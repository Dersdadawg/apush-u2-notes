There were two main tax supported churches in the colonies by 1755: the [[Congregational Church]] (to which [[Puritans]] belonged to) and the Anglican Church ([[Church of England]]).

![[Pasted image 20260905182633.png|378]]

The Anglican Church became the official faith in [[Georgia]], the [[Carolinas]], [[Virginia]], [[Maryland]], and part of [[New York]]. It was a major source of influence from the King. It had a bad reputation.

The Congregational Church grew out of the Puritans, and was established (tax-funded) in all [[New England]] colonies except [[Rhode Island]]. Massachusetts taxed all members but later excluded members of other denominations. [[Presbyterianism]] was not made official in any colonies.

Pres. and Cong. both harbored revolutionary ideas to break away from the King's authority and the Anglican Church, while the Anglicans remained loyal to the king.

Compared to Europe, the colonies as a whole offered relatively broad religious tolerance, though established churches and periodic persecution (of Catholics, Quakers, and other dissenters) still existed in many colonies.

The [[Great Awakening]] revitalized American religion.
