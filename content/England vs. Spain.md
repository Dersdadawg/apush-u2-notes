Spain and England were allies in the 1400s, but several factors led to their relations souring: for one, the [[English Protestant Reformation]] under [[King Henry VIII]] led to religious schism. For a while, Catholicism and Protestantism battled it out in England among the [[Tudors]], with both sides scoring minor victories, but under [[Queen Elizabeth I]] the country was unified under Protestantism.

Under Elizabeth, the English plundered the Spanish and also spread Protestantism in the New World. A number of semi-piratical "sea dogs" gained wealth and fame, such as [[Francis Drake]].

The Spanish were succeeding. They had amassed a massive fleet of ships called the [[Spanish Armada]] under [[Philip II]], the Spanish monarch. However, the English sea dogs defeated them, resulting in a decisive victory for them in 1588. This was the beginning of the end for the Spanish (see [[Decline of Spain]]).

This victory effectively reversed the role of Spain and England, since England was now unified under a charismatic monarch, possessed superior naval prowess, and also had a relatively unified people. The reign of Elizabeth was a Golden Age for the English (see [[Elizabethan Era]]).

The [[Anglo-Spanish War]] came to an end in 1604 with the signing of the [[Treaty of London]].
