### Settlement and early years
In 1606, two years after the Anglo-Spanish War ended, the [[Virginia Company]], a joint-stock company, founded Jamestown after receiving a charter from [[King James I]] of England for gold. Their motivations were gold and to find a passage through the Americas to the Indies. The charter was important because it guaranteed the same rights to the Jamestown settlers and eventually to subsequent colonies, as were bequeathed to native Englishmen (see [[Colonial Charters]]). However, these rights also led to the [[Anglicization of British Colonies]]. They landed in 1607 in [[Virginia]] near the mouth of the [[Chesapeake Bay]].

The early years of Jamestown were horrible. Most of the original colonists died within the first year from starvation and disease, a relief expedition was wrecked in Bermuda in 1609, and the settlers were too occupied with prospecting for gold to look for food in the abundant woods around them.

[[John Smith]] was captured by the Indians in 1607, but the Chief Powhatan's daughter [[Pocahontas]] saved him. Smith saved the colony when he took over its leadership in 1608, declaring "He who shall not work shall not eat."

From 1609-1610, there was an intense period of winter called the Starving Time. Only 60 out of 400 initial settlers to have reached Virginia survived.

### Wars with Indians

Around the Jamestown lived a loose confederacy of Indians called Powhatan's Confederacy which they just called Powhatan after their chief Powhatan.

In 1610, a new governor called [[Lord De La Warr]] took over, who ordered the settlers back to Jamestown as they were trying to leave back to England, and took military action against the Indians.

This led to the [[First Anglo-Powhatan War]] which ran from 1610-1614 before being ended by Pocahontas's marriage to [[John Rolfe]].

There was a fragile peace between them for some 8 years, but the Indians rebelled against the land-hungry colonists in 1622, beginning the [[Second Anglo-Powhatan War]]. These attacks left 347 settlers dead. The Virginia Company decreed the settlers to wipe out the Indians. The ensuing violence from the settlers pushed the Indian survivors westward.

The Indians rebelled again in the [[Third Anglo-Powhatan War]] in 1644. They were defeated, and in the peace treaty signed in 1646, they were confined to a defined boundary and made tributary to the English crown — an early precursor to later, much more systematic forms of forced Native confinement.

Despite these negative effects, Indian tribes like the Powhatans demonstrated resourcefulness and creativity by using the Europeans to finesse guns out of them, though this led to Indian-on-Indian violence.
