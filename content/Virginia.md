Founded by [[Jamestown]] settlers in 1607.

John Rolfe, who married Pocahontas, pioneered Virginia's economy. He farmed [[Tobacco]], and by 1612 he perfected it. The European demand skyrocketed from Rolfe's innovative techniques, and soon the colonists hungered for more land to expand their tobacco farming operation, since tobacco was detrimental to the soil. This led to tensions with the Indians.

It also led to a demand for more land, which led to slavery. In 1619, a Dutch warship landed in Virginia and sold the colony about 20 Africans. However, for the time being, enslaved Africans were too costly to import en masse to Virginia, so planters depended mainly on [[Indentured Servants]]. Those servants and other landless colonists later rebelled in 1676 in [[Bacon's Rebellion]], driven partly by the government's refusal to let them expand onto western land.

Representative self-government was born with the [[House of Burgesses]] in 1619. The Virginia Company authorized the settlers to form an assembly to decide matters themselves. However, increasingly James I detested tobacco and the House of Burgesses, leading to his decision to revoke the colonial charter in 1624.
