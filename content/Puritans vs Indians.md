English settlers frequently clashed with Indians, such as in the [[First Anglo-Powhatan War]] and [[Second Anglo-Powhatan War]], [[Yamasee War]], [[Tuscarora War]], etc.

The Indians and English got on well enough at first, with the [[Wampanoag]] Indians, facilitated by [[Squanto]], helping the pilgrims survive the winter.

The Wampanoag chief Massasoit signed a peace treaty with Plymouth in the spring of 1621, laying the groundwork for the peaceful [[Thanksgiving]] feast that autumn.

As colonists pushed deeper inland to the [[Connecticut]] River Valley, which was sacred to the [[Pequot]] Indians, they burned wigwams and shot the Indians, leading to the [[Pequot War]] from 1636-1638.

The Indians struck back when Massasoit's son, Metacom, known as King Phillip among the English, coordinated attacks on the English settlements in 1675 after forging an alliance between Indian tribes. This was [[King Phillip's War]], which ended in 1676 with the Indians losing and Metacom's head being mounted on a pike in Plymouth.
