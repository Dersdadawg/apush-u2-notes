## Primary Education
[[Puritans]] emphasized reading the bible, leading them to create the first tax-supported schools; Massachusetts's 1647 "Old Deluder Satan Act" required towns to fund schools, though in practice these primarily educated boys. In the [[Middle Colonies]] schools were church-sponsored or private, in [[Southern Colonies]] children got whatever education their family could arrange.

## Higher Education
First colleges in the US were designed to create preachers and clergy
- [[Harvard]] in 1636 to prepare ministers by Puritans
- Anglicans ([[Church of England]]) opened College of William and Mary in 1693
- Congregationalists ([[Congregational Church]]) started Yale in [[Connecticut]] in 1701.
- Only nonsectarian school was UPenn founded by [[Benjamin Franklin]].
## Med School
Doctors were not very good, doctor's training was to be an apprentice to an experienced physician, College of Philadelphia was first medical college in 1765, part of Franklin's idea

## Law School
Lawyers formed bars, eventually argued for colonial rights — figures like John Adams, James Otis, and Patrick Henry (all Unit 3 Revolutionary-era figures) trained this way.
