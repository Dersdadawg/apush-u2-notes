Moderate Puritans sensing impending doom after anti-Puritan reforms proposed after the suspension of Parliament in 1629 by [[King Charles I]] secured a royal charter and founded the Massachusetts Bay Colony.

They set off on an expedition in 1630, carrying 11 ships and nearly a thousand immigrants. This contributed to the [[Great English Migration]] of the 1630s.

About 20,000 came to Massachusetts.

[[John Winthrop]] became the first governor of the MBC. He believed he received a calling from God to lead the religious experiment and led the colony as governor or deputy-governor for 19 years. He wrote [[A Model of Christian Charity]], outlining his vision for the colony to be a "City on a Hill".

The colony was dominated by the [[Congregational Church]]. Men not in the church enjoyed no voting priveliges.

The MBC was not democratic in this sense, since only churchgoing freemen who were "visible saints" could elect governors and representatives.

Eventually, the social harmony accorded to by the common religion was upended by groups such as the [[Quakers]]. They openly denied the authority of the Puritan authority, and they were persecuted through fines and flogging, some even being hung. This led them to found the colony of [[Pennsylvania]].

Challenge to Puritan authority also came through figures such as [[Anne Hutchinson]], and [[Roger Williams]]. 

As punishment for increasing colonial defiance, [[King Charles II]] revoked colonial charter in 1684.

Charter was given back in 1691, but now as a royal charter. Voting rights were given to all landowning men, not just churchgoers.
