The English also tried to settle the West Indies, an area the Spanish had long dominated; Spain's overstretched resources — worn down partly by the [[Eighty Years' War]] and its broader [[Decline of Spain]] — gave England the opening to move in.

They won [[Jamaica]] in 1655. The West Indies' economy was based on sugar. However, while tobacco required just a little to be planted to return profits, sugar required large swaths to generate a return. The land would have to be cleared, and the sugar had to be processed. All of this led to large labor demands, which could only be met by wealthy planters.

Since they had the means to do so, they imported African slaves, more than 250,000 from 1640-1700. By the turn of the 18th century, enslaved blacks outnumbered whites 4:1 in the English West Indies.

To control the slaves, they came up with legal codes granting them virtually no rights. The [[Barbados Slave Code]] of 1661, devised in [[Barbados]], was particularly brutal.

Several poor farmers squeezed out by the sugar barons came to [[Carolinas|South Carolina]] in 1670, bringing with them the practices of the slave trade. Carolina adopted a version of the code in 1696, leading other colonies to do the same.
