Georgia was founded in 1733. It was set up as a buffer colony against the French in [[Louisiana]] and the Spanish in [[Spanish Florida]].

To aid in this purpose of staving off wars, it received monetary subsidies from the British government. It was named after [[King George II]], and founded by philanthropists. They wanted Georgia to be a haven for debtors.

The Trustees hoped Georgia would produce silk and wine, but both ventures largely failed; the colony eventually turned to rice, like [[Carolinas|South Carolina]], especially after slavery was legalized in 1750.

[[James Oglethorpe]] was a major figure in Georgia, repelling Spanish attacks from the south and pushing for prison reform for debtors.

Savannah became a melting pot, and all denominations except Catholics enjoyed toleration. It produced figures like [[John Wesley]] that founded the [[Methodist Church]].
