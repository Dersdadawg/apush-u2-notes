After the [[Eighty Years' War]], the [[Netherlands]] gained independence from the [[Spanish Empire]] controlled by the [[Habsburg Dynasty]].

This marked the start of the Dutch Golden Age, and the Netherlands rapidly expanded its colonial holdings.

[[Dutch East India Company]], large fleets, many men, became very rich

They wanted to gain control of the New World as well, so they hired [[Henry Hudson]] to explore. He sailed into [[Delaware]] Bay and [[New York]] Bay in **1609** to find the [[Northwest Passage]].

Later, in 1623-24, the [[Dutch West India Company]], which was less rich than it's Eastern counterpart but had recently looted some Spanish ships for money established New Netherlands.

It was established as a fur trading outpost.

The company bought Manhattan Island from the Natives for a small sum and some gifts.

New Amsterdam was New Netherland's capital and now New York. It was run by governors appointed by the company who were often dictatorial and despotic.

The state religion of the [[Dutch Reformed Church]] was the law of the land and other groups like [[Quakers]] and Catholics were discriminated against.

Poor treatment of local Native peoples — most notoriously Director Willem Kieft's unprovoked 1643 attack on the Lenape — triggered years of retaliatory war. A defensive wall was later built across Manhattan in 1653, primarily against a feared English attack, giving Wall Street its name.

At the same time, [[New England]] was adversarial to the expanding Dutch settlement in the New World. They were also contested by [[New Sweden]] on the Delaware River from 1638 to 1655, founded during Sweden's 17th-century rise as a great power that began under [[Gustavus Adolphus]].

Eventually they were kicked out under Governor General [[Peter Stuyvesant]].

But with British colonies to the north and south, as well as New Amsterdam being flooded with English colonists, the Dutch position seemed precarious. This danger precipitated when in 1664 [[King Charles II]] granted New Netherlands to his brother, the Duke of York. England thus won New Netherlands and named it New York.
