Swiss theologian [[John Calvin]] was a Protestant who pioneered the doctrine of [[Predestination]], the belief that God already knows who goes to heaven and who goes to hell.

This led to the development of Calvinism, which had several unique characteristics.

It left many faithful in want of signs that they are destined for heaven in their lives. This arguably culminated in the [[Protestant Work Ethic]], a phenomenon detailed by sociologist [[Max Weber]], where adherents tried to accumulate material wealth and saw that as a sign of God's favor during this life and the next.

They also saw the ability to live a faithful life as a sign of **conversion**, or God's saving grace. This led to the formation of groups like the Puritans (see [[Puritans]]), which tried to lead ultra-devout lives.
