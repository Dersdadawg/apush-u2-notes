The Connecticut River saw lots of English and Dutch settlers to migrate there, leading to the founding of Hartford in **1636**.

Reverend Thomas Hooker led a group of Boston Puritans to the Connecticut area.

In 1639, the settlers drafted the [[Fundamental Orders]]

In 1638, [[New Haven]] was founded by more devout Puritans trying to establish a theocracy, but they fell into disfavor with [[King Charles II]] because they sheltered two of the judges that put his father [[King Charles I]] to death.

This led New Haven to merge with the larger Connecticut valley communities into a single colony in 1662–1665. Connecticut remained a separate colony for the rest of the colonial period, aside from a brief three years (1686–1689) when it was folded into the [[Dominion of New England]].
