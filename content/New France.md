France was a latecomer to settling the New World because of internal wars, especially religious wars between the Catholics and French Protestants, known as [[Huguenots]].

For example, the [[St. Bartholomew's Day Massacre]] stands out as an example of Huguenot persecution. But in **1598,** the [[Edict of Nantes]] was signed which granted limited toleration to Protestants.

17th century France thrived after the wars of religion, especially during the reign of [[Louis XIV]] (1643-1715).

The first French settlement in the New World was [[Quebec]], on the banks of the [[St. Lawrence River]]. It was established by [[Samuel de Champlain]].

The French, unlike the British and the Spanish, built cooperative relationships with local Native nations, allying with the [[Huron]] and other Algonquian-speaking peoples against their rivals, the [[Iroquis Confederacy]]. This alliance, dating to early clashes like Champlain's 1609 battle against the Iroquois, fed the lasting hostility behind the [[Beaver Wars]], in which the Iroquois ultimately devastated the Huron in the 1640s.

Like [[New Netherlands]], the government of New France was initially headed by companies, but they failed and ownership was soon transferred to the king. As expected, it was basically a theocracy with few democratic rights afforded to its citizens.

The population of New France did not grow as fast as the other colonies, as French peasants had no real economic incentive to move to New France when there were more attractive options in the [[French Carribean]] colonies, and Protestant Huguenots who hoped to escape religious persecution were disbarred from moving to Catholic New France.

New France's principal economic activity was the [[Fur Trade]], carried out by *coureurs de bois*, as beaver pelts proved valuable in making various articles of clothing to trade in mainland Europe.

The fur trade inadvertently fueled French expansion deeper into the North American continent because beaver populations declined and the hunters pushed inland to meet demand.

They explored the Great Lakes and eventually the Rockies.

The hunters decimated the beaver populations, killed lots of Natives with white man's diseases, and greatly threatened their spiritual beliefs.

Catholic missionaries in New France, specifically [[Jesuits]], tried to convert Native Americans.

[[Robert de La Salle]] navigated the [[Mississippi River]] in **1682**, claiming the entire valley for France as Louisiana. A later expedition meant to found a colony near the Gulf of Mexico went astray into Spanish-claimed territory, and La Salle was killed by his own mutinous men in **1687**; his wayward Texas venture ironically spurred Spain to begin colonizing Texas to secure its claim against French encroachment. To contest English and Iroquois influence in the Great Lakes, [[Antoine Cadillac]] founded [[Detroit]] in **1701**.

To further counter the Spanish, they planted forts in [[Louisiana]], the most important of which was [[New Orleans]], founded in **1718**.
