There were several motivating factors for the English colonizing the New World. First was population growth. The population of England was scarcely 3 million in 1550, but blew up to 4 million in 1600. Landlords were enclosing land for sheepfarming, leading peasant farmers to farm on increasingly little land.

Another factor was religious discrimination. [[Puritans]],an ultra-devout Calvinist denomination, was persecuted by the mainline [[Church of England]].

Many colonists were also the second or third oldest brothers in the family, as the English law of **primogeniture** ensured that only the eldest sons could inherit land.

However, later innovations such as the [[Joint-stock Company]] allowed people to invest in expeditions without having to absorb all of the losses since capital was pooled.
