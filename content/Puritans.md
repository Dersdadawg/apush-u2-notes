Puritans were Calvinists ([[Calvinism]]) who led ultra-devout lives, as a sign of **conversion**, or God's grace leading them to heaven.

They lived in the woolen districts of London, and were discontent with the [[Church of England]].

[[King Henry VIII]] was content to keep some Catholic traditions, but they wanted to see the church wholly de-Catholicized.

They also believed that only "visible" saints should be allowed to go to church, but the church enrolled all the kings subject, which led to the emergence of the Puritan Separatists, who wanted to leave the church entirely.

[[King James I]], head of state and church from 1603-1625, threatened to harass the Separatists further if they made more trouble, since it threatened both his royal and spiritual authority.

In 1608, they left for the Netherlands, who were majority Calvinist. But they dreaded the Dutchification of their children and wanted to practice their religion while living as Englishmen.

This led to the pilgrimage of [[Plymouth]].

The Separatists were an extreme faction. More moderate Puritans sought to reform the Church of England from within. However, when [[King Charles I]] dismissed Parliament in 1629 and passed adversarial reforms to Puritans proposed by reactionary Archbishop William Laud, this encouraged more Puritans to move to the New World.
