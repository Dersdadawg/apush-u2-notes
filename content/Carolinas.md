The [[English Civil War]] from 1642 to 1651, led to a period of relaxed colonial expansion since domestic matters took precedence. However, with the ascension of Charles II in 1660, the colonial engine revved on with renewed fervor.

He granted eight of his court favorites that helped him gain back the throne the Carolinas in 1670, in order to grow food to provision the sugar trade in Barbados.

The settlers were in many cases from Barbados, and brought its slave trade with them. They mainly captured and enslaved Indians, with the help of the Savannah Indians as slave-raiding partners. The Carolinas also traded slaves to other colonies, even as north as the [[New England]] colonies.

By 1707, however, the Savannah Indians defected to [[Pennsylvania]], but the Carolinians wiped them out by 1710. At this time rice emerged as a major export. But they needed Africans experienced in rice-growing to cultivate the crops, but these were expensive.

Charles Town (later Charleston) became a major port city. Carolina's frontier was plagued with war with the Spanish Floridians (see [[Spanish Florida]]).

On the north border, lots of Virginians of questionable moral character emigrated down. These "squatters" farmed their own tobacco and practiced their own customs and religions. They developed a bad reputation, their poverty and independence contrasted with their aristocratic of Virginia and South Carolina to the north and south. After much friction, they separated in 1712, and both segments became a royal colony.

The North Carolinians defeated the Tuscarora Indians in the [[Tuscarora War]] from 1711-1715. They eventually became the sixth nation in the [[Iroquis Confederacy]].

The SCs defeated the Yamasee in the [[Yamasee War]] from 1715-1717.

These two tribes were basically wiped out, but the [[Cherokee]], [[Creek]], and [[Iroquis Confederacy]] kept the British out of the west.
