In the early 18th century, religion in America was less intense than it had been in the century before. [[Puritans]] and Congregationalists were losing members because of their orthodox requirements and liberalized their sermons to compensate, much to the ire of more orthodox clergy.

Developments of ideas such as [[Arminianism]] were threatening to the [[Calvinism]] establishment.

Preachers such as [[Jonathan Edwards]] and [[George Whitefield]] delivered charismatic sermons that swept the colonies up in a religious fervor. Their sermons focused on themes of divine omnipotence and human helplessness.

There was a broader conflict between the **old lights** and **new lights** of the faith. The old lights were skeptical of the emotional theatrics of the new lights.

It also led to the founding of several eminent institutions for the training of new preachers such as Princeton, Brown, Rutgers, and Dartmouth.
